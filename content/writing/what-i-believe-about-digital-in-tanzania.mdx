If you've made it to the memos, you're either curious, thorough, or lost. All three are welcome. This is the longer version of the site: who I am, how I got here, and where I'm trying to go.

## How I got here

I studied Computer Science at the University of Dar es Salaam, did internships at the e-Government Authority and Oceanware, and in November 2022 joined Simplitech as an intern. My first jobs were small: design documents for user journeys, a Contact Us page, bug fixes from user feedback. Not glamorous, but it taught me the thing that still drives my work today. Every feature is a conversation with a real person on the other side.

By August 2023 I was a Software Engineer. By May 2024 I was Tech Lead. Somewhere in between I led an external consultant through building our first mobile app, then took it over full time. That's where the job stopped being "write the code" and became "decide what's worth building, make sure it gets built right, and make sure it actually helps someone."

Today Simplify serves businesses across Tanzania. I lead it across web, mobile, API and WhatsApp, which in a startup means I also do product, design, partner integrations, customer conversations and a fair amount of explaining. I'm also leading our research into AI for audit work, which is the most fun I've had with a problem in a while.

## What I've learned about the job

The title says Tech Lead. The work is closer to translator.

The business says "we need to grow." The customer says "this screen confuses me." The regulator says "this field is mandatory." The developer says "that'll take three weeks." My job is to hear all four and turn them into one decision that holds together.

That's what I mean when I say I work in the overlap of product, tech, market, organisation and business. None of them alone builds a good product. Together, they usually do.

## What I believe about digital in Tanzania

We have a lot of digital work that exists to tick a box. Websites that describe instead of sell. Apps copied from somewhere else. Systems built for the procurement process instead of the person using them.

I think we can do better, and I think the biggest opportunity is in the places almost everyone touches: banks, telecoms, payments, and public services. When those get digital right, millions of people feel it. When they get it wrong, millions of people queue.

I also believe AI will change finance and compliance work across East Africa, and that it should be built here, with our rules and our context, not bolted on later.

## Where I'm heading

Short term: keep building products people actually use, and keep getting better at the strategy side of the work.

Longer term: lead digital and product strategy at scale, somewhere with real reach. Advise organisations on getting their product and digital direction right. And sit on boards, especially ones focused on youth and innovation, where this kind of thinking is often missing from the room.

I don't want to be the person who receives the decision. I want to help shape it.
